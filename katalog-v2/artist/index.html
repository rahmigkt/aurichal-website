The artist’s story

An Art Practice Shaped by Landscape, Light, and Clay

“I am interested in what remains when a landscape becomes a memory: a color, a line, the weight of the earth.”

Elena Varela grew up between Cuenca’s Andean landscape and her grandmother’s pottery workshop. Early memories of mineral earth, shifting mountain light, and objects made slowly by hand continue to inform every work.

After studying fine arts in Madrid, Elena returned to Ecuador and developed a restrained visual language in mineral pigment, raw linen, stoneware, and iron oxide. Her paintings and ceramics share the same concern: how surface, weight, and silence can change the feeling of a room.

Inside the practice

Materials That Hold Time and Place

Each work begins with a physical material and the patience to let its natural irregularities remain visible.

Mineral pigment

Colour Drawn From the Earth

Thin layers of rust, ochre, charcoal, and chalk build atmospheric fields that shift with natural light.

Raw linen

Surface as Part of the Composition

Visible linen grain gives the paintings warmth, depth, and a tactile architectural presence.

Hand-built clay

Forms That Preserve the Hand

Coiling, pinching, and mineral slips leave small asymmetries that make every ceramic work singular.
